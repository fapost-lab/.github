# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they meant well. Argue about the code, not about
each other.

## What we expect

This project is worked on by people in different countries, time zones and first
languages, at very different levels of experience. That is an asset, and it only
works if participation is comfortable for all of them.

So, in issues, pull requests, reviews and any other project space:

- **Critique the work, not the person.** "This query runs per row" is a review.
  "You clearly don't know SQL" is not.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** A question that looks naive is usually
  missing context, not intelligence. Supply the context.
- **Accept that maintainers say no.** A rejected pull request is a decision about
  scope or direction, not a verdict on you. Reasons will be given; repeating the
  request louder will not change them.
- **Write for someone whose English is their third language.** Plain sentences,
  no idioms where a plain word exists, no sarcasm that inverts your meaning.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form: sexualised language or imagery; insults, slurs, or
attacks on someone's identity — including nationality, ethnicity, religion,
gender, sexual orientation, disability, age or appearance; sustained disruption;
deliberate intimidation; publishing anyone's private information without their
explicit permission; and unwelcome attention of any kind after being asked to
stop.

Political argument unrelated to the project belongs elsewhere. This is a
technical space with contributors on many sides of many things, and it stays
usable by staying on topic.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — repositories, issues, pull requests,
discussions, and any channel that represents the project — and to conduct
elsewhere that is directed at a participant *because* of their participation
here.

## Reporting

Report to **[conduct@fapost.in](mailto:conduct@fapost.in)**.

Reports are read by the maintainers and kept confidential. Tell us what happened,
where, and when; links help. You will get an acknowledgement, and you will be
told what was decided.

If your report concerns a maintainer, say so — it will be handled by someone
else, or, if that is not possible given the size of the project, by an
independent third party we will name to you before proceeding.

## Enforcement

What happens depends on what occurred and whether it is a pattern:

1. **A private word** — an explanation of what was wrong, and why.
2. **A warning** — with a defined period of no contact with the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Steps can be skipped. A first offence that is severe enough goes straight to the
last one.
